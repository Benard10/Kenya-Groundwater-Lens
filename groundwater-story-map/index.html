START HERE · WHY THIS MATTERS

A borehole may sit on private land. The groundwater is a shared resource.

How can mapped records help Kenya protect that resource—and decide where verification should begin?

Kenya's water resources are held by the national government in trust for the public. WRA regulates their use. This map helps reveal where records are concentrated, where evidence is thin and which entries deserve a closer look.

WHAT THIS TOOL IS FOR

PRIVATE LAND · SHARED WATER

Drilling a borehole is not simply a private construction project.

A borehole draws from an aquifer that may extend beyond one boundary. Abstraction can affect neighbouring water users, rivers, wetlands and the long-term condition of the resource.

That is why WRA's process connects construction, water use and accountability. The exact requirements and charges depend on the use category and current regulations.

Check WRA's permitting process ↗Water Resources Regulations, 2025 ↗

  1. 01
    Before construction

    Apply to WRA and obtain the required approval or authorisation before constructing abstraction works.

  2. 02
    Before abstraction

    Complete the required records and checks. Authorisation to construct does not by itself permit water abstraction.

  3. 03
    While using water

    Follow permit conditions, measure and report use where required, and pay the applicable permit fees and water-use charges.

  4. This dashboard cannot show whether a mapped source has completed these steps. Check WRA's official records before drawing a compliance conclusion.

THE LIST BEHIND THE MAP

Each dot began as a survey record.

Surveyors visited places, found a water source and wrote down what they could see or confirm. The dashboard turns that list into a map.

The list contains 21,284 mapped water-source records across 40 counties. It is a useful place to start, but it is not a complete list of every source in Kenya.

THE CATCH

A map of records is not a map of water.

Think about restaurant reviews. A street with many reviews does not always have the most restaurants. It may simply have more people writing reviews.

This map works in a similar way. A bright cluster may show that an area was surveyed more often. A quiet area may simply have fewer mapped records.

Keep this in mind
Bright means more mapped records, not more water. Quiet means less mapped information, not no water.

HOW TO READ WHAT YOU SEE

Every mark gives a clue, not a final answer.

Bright cluster

What it shows: Many mapped records are close together.

What it does not show: It does not prove that the area has more groundwater.

Quiet or blank area

What it shows: This dataset has fewer nearby mapped records.

What it does not show: It does not prove that groundwater is absent.

Depth bar, line or column

What it shows: The recorded depth at which a source was built.

What it does not show: It does not show the water table, water quality or how much water is available.

Missing depth

What it shows: The record does not include a usable depth value.

What it does not show: It does not mean the source has zero depth, is shallow or has a problem.

Single dot

What it shows: One mapped source record in the public dataset.

What it does not show: It does not tell us who owns the source, whether it has a permit, or whether anything is wrong.

QUESTION THE DATA TOO

A precise number can still be a question.

In the Lake Victoria region of Busia, two entries record construction depths of 300 metres and two nearby entries record 280 metres.

Those depths may be correct. They may also reflect repeated records, units, an old entry or another issue. The map cannot decide. The original survey form, borehole completion record, permit information, local geology and a site check can.

WRA'S ROLE

Turn a signal into verified water-resource information.

Under Kenya's Water Act, WRA regulates the management and use of water resources, handles water-permit applications and conditions, collects and shares water-resource information, and checks whether permit conditions are followed.

This dashboard can help WRA and its partners prioritise record checks, compare public entries with official files, plan field visits and ask source owners or operators for missing evidence.

It cannot replace WRA's records, technical assessment or field verification.

WRA mandate ↗Water Act, sections 12–13 ↗

WHY THE GAPS MATTER

Missing information can show us where to start.

Gaps do not make the map useless. They show where information is strong and where it is thin.

The Water Resources Authority (WRA), county teams, researchers and source operators can use these gaps to plan record checks, field visits and future surveys.

WHEN RECORDS CHANGED

An update date is not a drilling date.

The timeline shows when a database record was last updated. Peaks may come from a large data update.

The date does not tell us when a source was drilled, when it began operating or whether it is still active.

A SHARED CHECKING PROCESS

A dot on the map is a clue. Checking turns it into a fact.

The map can help WRA and its partners choose what to check first. Counties, data publishers, source owners and operators can help confirm what each dot represents.

  1. 01
    Spot it

    Pick a dot or pattern that needs a closer look.

  2. 02
    Find the record

    Look for the source in WRA's records and trace the original survey entry.

  3. 03
    Compare the papers

    Match it with available permits, applications, borehole logs and completion records.

  4. 04
    Visit the site

    Confirm the source, its condition, who uses it and any details that need checking.

  5. 05
    Confirm or correct

    Keep a supported record, correct it where needed and note what remains uncertain.

THE DECISION THIS MAP SUPPORTS

Where should WRA and its partners verify first?

The dashboard reveals where public records are concentrated, where information is thin and which values deserve a closer look. Its purpose is to prioritise verification—not declare groundwater abundance or judge compliance. Official records and fieldwork turn those signals into evidence.
